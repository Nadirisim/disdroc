Behind disdroc

Who we are

disdroc isn't a company. It's a solo, self-hosted project — built, run and paid for by one person under the name nadirisim — that grew from a personal side project into something real people use every day.

The short version

  • nadirisim — one developer, not a company, no investors.
  • It runs on hardware we own, not a rented cloud.
  • The client — everything you can see and audit — is going public on GitHub.
  • A few server-side pieces (auth, anti-abuse, moderation) stay closed on purpose — see below.

01 One person, not a company

There's no team, no office and no funding round behind disdroc — just nadirisim, building and running it as a hobby that turned into a real, working chat platform. Every feature, every bug fix and every 2am outage response has come from the same person. That's also why the tone of this site is honest about what disdroc is: a passion project, not a startup pitch.

You can find nadirisim's other work and this project's source on GitHub: github.com/nadirisim.

02 Why we're opening the source

This site already claims no ads, no tracking and no data selling. Those claims shouldn't have to be taken on faith — they should be checkable. Opening the client source means anyone can read exactly what disdroc sends, stores and requests, line by line, instead of trusting a paragraph on a privacy page. It also means people can report bugs against real code, suggest features with a patch attached, and see how a small self-hosted app is actually built.

03 What's public, and what stays closed

disdroc is open enough to read and learn from, not open enough to hand out a blueprint for breaking it. Concretely:

Public

The full web client, the desktop app and the Android app — every screen, every request they make. Also the Bot API used by third-party bots, documented in the developer docs.

Closed on purpose

Server-side authentication, session security, moderation/anti-abuse detection and rate-limiting. Publishing exact thresholds and internals there wouldn't help anyone use disdroc — it would just be a how-to guide for breaking in or dodging the safety filters.

Think of it like a lock manufacturer publishing how their doors are hung, but not the keyway blueprints. The account data behind the live service (messages, files, the user database) is never part of this either way — open source describes the code, not a copy of everyone's conversations.

04 What it's built with

Deliberately small and boring, on purpose — a solo project has to stay maintainable by one person.

Node.js + Express Socket.IO WebRTC voice/video SQLite Vanilla JS frontend llama.cpp (local AI) Self-hosted, own hardware
